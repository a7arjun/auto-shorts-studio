# Pokémon battle storytelling

The user requested this direction on 24 October 2026. Apply it to new videos rather than changing already uploaded or accepted YouTube schedules.

## Story and voice
Write a compact fictional fan battle with three clear exchanges (opening, counter, payoff). Use six distinct action scenes, usually two per round. Open on the action within the first second. Examples: Pikachu charges Thunderbolt; Lucario dodges; the dodge creates an Iron Tail opening; Aura Sphere or Bone Rush forces a new response. Use move names deliberately, with short clauses and varied verbs. Remove repetitive 'used attack', long introductions, HP percentages and repeated winner summaries. Spend the saved time on a second or third tactical exchange. Do not resolve every episode with the same generic question. Give a clear tactical payoff or a deliberate, varied cliffhanger. Stay near 25–40 seconds where the story fits; never rush narration solely to hit a fixed speed. Narration, captions, move labels and images must describe the same action in order.

These are invented fan-battle stories. Label both the video and description 'Fictional fan battle'. Do not attach measured trial counts, percentages, level-100 odds, canon victories or official footage claims to invented dodges and outcomes. If a future request is explicitly analytical, use the existing simulation route and preserve every real event instead of fabricating it.

## Art and motion
Use built-in image generation for new raster art; do not require a paid API key, billing or hosting. Generate a consistent six-panel storyboard per episode, with recognizable characters, appropriate moves, expressive poses and a complete action sequence. Inspect all panels before rendering. Default to the cinematic painted-anime treatment; the user is reviewing alternate neon-comic, watercolor, clay-diorama and pixel-art samples. Generated images are illustrations; use camera moves, scene cuts and modest energy effects, and describe the result honestly as a motion comic rather than frame-by-frame character animation. Choose motion to suit the medium. Avoid ornate statistics overlays that compete with the fight. Keep vertical 720×1280 or better, readable captions and both characters inside the picture. Use locally rendered Kokoro speech and original or properly licensed audio only.
